**Prepared for:** External validator review
**Date:** 2026-07-20
**Purpose:** Independent classification of 29 unresolved conceptual tensions in the foundations of physics.

---

## §0. Instructions for Validators

Thank you for participating in this independent review. This package presents 29 conceptual tensions ("schisms") identified in the foundations of physics. Each schism represents a bifurcation where the physics community has not reached consensus on which path is correct.

### Your Task

For each schism, please classify it according to three dimensions:

| Dimension | Options | Description |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| **A. Category** | Physics / Philosophy / Both | Is this primarily a physics question (empirically resolvable), a philosophical question (conceptual/interpretive), or both? |
| **B. Resolution status** | Unresolved / Contested / Resolved / Ill-posed | Is the community divided (unresolved), actively debating (contested), largely settled (resolved), or is the question itself misformulated (ill-posed)? |
| **C. Confidence** | High / Medium / Low | How confident are you in your classification? |

Additionally:

- **Missing schisms:** Are there unresolved tensions you believe should be in this taxonomy but aren't?
- **General comments:** Any overarching observations about the taxonomy's structure, framing, or completeness.

### Time Estimate

2–4 hours total. You are not expected to classify every schism — classify only those within your expertise. Estimate: 10–15 schisms per validator in 2 hours.

### Response Format

Please return your classifications in any format (marked-up PDF, spreadsheet, email reply). A structured classification form is provided in §3.

## §1. The 29 Schisms

Schisms are organized into five layers, from mathematical substrate to epistemology.

---

### Layer 1: Mathematical Substrate

*Fundamental assumptions about what physical theories are built from.*

| S# | Schism | Description |
|----|--------|-------------|
| **S1** | Continuum vs. Discrete | Is physical reality fundamentally continuous (real numbers, differential equations) or discrete (finite, combinatorial)? Current theories use both without resolving which is primitive. |
| **S3** | Background Independence | Does physics require a pre-existing spacetime stage (Newton, special relativity) or must spacetime itself be generated by the theory (general relativity, quantum gravity)? |
| **S9** | Discovered vs. Invented | Are mathematical structures "discovered" (Platonism: they exist independently) or "invented" (constructivism: we create them)? Affects whether laws of physics are out there or in here. |
| **S14** | Which Representation Space? | Should physics use Hilbert spaces (quantum), Minkowski spacetime (relativity), phase space (classical), or something else? Is there a single "correct" representation, or are multiple valid? |
| **S18** | Classical Logic Assumption | Does physics require classical Boolean logic, or should it use a different logical framework (quantum logic, constructivist logic, paraconsistent logic)? |
| **S28** | Consistency vs. Completeness | Can a physical theory be both consistent (no contradictions) and complete (describes everything)? Gödel's theorems suggest mathematical limits — do they apply to physics? |

---

### Layer 2: Ontology of States and Laws

*What exists, and how does it change?*

| S# | Schism | Description |
|----|--------|-------------|
| **S2** | State vs. Process | Is physics fundamentally about states (configurations at an instant) or processes (transformations between configurations)? Quantum mechanics and relativity pull in different directions. |
| **S7** | Determinism vs. Indeterminism | Are physical outcomes uniquely determined by prior states, or is there irreducible randomness? Quantum measurement outcomes appear probabilistic, but interpretations disagree on whether this is fundamental or epistemic. |
| **S12** | Fundamental vs. Emergent | Is there a "bottom level" of reality (fundamental particles/fields), or is everything emergent from lower-level dynamics all the way down? |
| **S13** | Fixed Constants vs. Evolving Parameters | Are the constants of nature (fine-structure constant, etc.) truly constant, or do they evolve? String theory's landscape suggests they might vary across the multiverse. |
| **S16** | Do Laws Exist? | Are there "laws of nature" in the sense of governing rules, or are regularities just patterns we observe? Humean vs. non-Humean debate. |
| **S19** | Laws vs. Initial Conditions | Are dynamical laws and initial/boundary conditions fundamentally separate categories, or is this distinction artificial? The "nomological dualism" problem. |
| **S21** | Objective State | Is there an observer-independent, objective state of a physical system, or is the state always relative to an observer or reference frame? |
| **S26** | Single Outcome vs. All Outcomes | Does measurement produce a single definite outcome (collapse), or do all possible outcomes occur (many-worlds)? The measurement problem in a nutshell. |

---

### Layer 3: Quantum-Classical Divide

*How do quantum and classical descriptions relate?*

| S# | Schism | Description |
|----|--------|-------------|
| **S4** | Arrow of Time | Why does time have a direction? The fundamental laws are time-symmetric, yet we observe irreversible processes. Is the arrow fundamental or emergent? |
| **S5** | Level Boundary (Heisenberg Cut) | Where exactly does quantum behavior give way to classical behavior? Is there a sharp boundary, a gradual transition, or is the distinction itself illusory? |
| **S6** | Non-Local Correlations | How do entangled particles exhibit correlations that seem to violate locality? Is the universe non-local, or is there a deeper explanation? |
| **S17** | Unique History vs. Many Histories | Does a physical system follow a single trajectory through time, or does it explore all possible histories (path integral formulation)? |
| **S20** | Dynamics vs. Kinematics | Should physics be formulated dynamically (equations of motion, initial values) or kinematically (constraints on what is possible, independent of time)? |
| **S22** | Reversibility vs. Irreversibility | Are fundamental physical processes reversible (unitary evolution), or is irreversibility built in at the deepest level? |

---

### Layer 4: Spacetime and Gravity

*The nature of space, time, and their quantum description.*

| S# | Schism | Description |
|----|--------|-------------|
| **S8** | Single Universe vs. Multiverse | Is our observable universe unique, or is it one of many in a broader multiverse? String theory landscape, eternal inflation, and many-worlds all suggest multiplicity. |
| **S11** | Problem of Time | In quantum gravity (Wheeler-DeWitt equation), time disappears from the formalism. Is time fundamental or emergent? How do we recover the experience of temporal flow? |
| **S15** | One Description vs. Many | Can all physical phenomena be described by a single theory (Theory of Everything), or are multiple, mutually irreducible descriptions necessary? |
| **S23** | Fixed Spacetime Dimensionality | Is the number of spacetime dimensions fixed (3+1), or can it vary? String theory requires 10 or 11 dimensions; some approaches suggest dimensional reduction at high energies. |
| **S24** | Vacuum as Trivial vs. Non-Trivial | Is the vacuum truly empty (zero particles, zero energy), or does it have structure (quantum fluctuations, non-zero energy density, hidden degrees of freedom)? |
| **S27** | Map vs. Territory | Is a physical theory a "map" (representation) of an underlying "territory" (reality), or is the distinction between description and described itself problematic? |

---

### Layer 5: Epistemology and the Observer

*Knowledge, observation, and the position of the describer.*

| S# | Schism | Description |
|----|--------|-------------|
| **S10** | Observer Inside vs. Outside | Can a physical theory be formulated from an external "God's eye" perspective, or must it acknowledge that the observer is inside the system being described? |
| **S25** | Omniscience vs. Finite Knowledge | Can any physical description be complete (capture all relevant information), or is there always a horizon beyond which knowledge is impossible? |
| **S29** | Explanation vs. Description | Does physics explain why things happen (causal mechanisms), or does it merely describe what happens (mathematical models)? The "shut up and calculate" debate. |

## §2. Background (Optional Reading)

*This section provides context but is not required for classification. You may skip it and proceed directly to §3.*

### What Generated These 29 Schisms?

A systematic scan of the foundations-of-physics literature (2025–2026) identified recurring bifurcations where:
- The community is genuinely divided (not just one heterodox view vs. consensus)
- The division affects theory construction (not merely interpretation)
- Resolution would meaningfully change how physics is done

The schisms are not randomly selected — they form a dependency stack. Layer 1 schisms (mathematical substrate) constrain Layer 2 (state/law ontology), which constrains Layer 3 (quantum-classical divide), etc. Resolution at a lower layer propagates upward.

### What "Resolution" Means

In this taxonomy, a schism is "resolved" not when one side is declared correct, but when a theoretical framework dissolves the bifurcation — showing that both sides of the apparent dichotomy are aspects of a single underlying structure. The framework does not choose between A and B; it shows that A and B are the same thing viewed from different perspectives.

This is not the same as "we've decided the answer" — it's "the question no longer arises."

### Relationship to This Research Program

The research program that produced this taxonomy proposes a specific framework for resolving these schisms. **You are NOT being asked to evaluate that framework.** Your task is to independently classify the schisms themselves. Whether you agree with the proposed resolution mechanism is irrelevant to this classification exercise.
