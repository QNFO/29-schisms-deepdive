## §0. Selection Criteria

Each candidate was evaluated against five criteria:

| Criterion | Threshold | Rationale |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| **C1: Foundation expertise** | Peer-reviewed publications in foundations of physics or philosophy of physics | Must be able to evaluate schism classification claims |
| **C2: No QNFO affiliation** | Must not be an author, collaborator, or known associate of QNFO | Independence requirement |
| **C3: Publication record** | At least one book or ≥ 5 papers in foundations | Demonstrates sustained engagement |
| **C4: Schism-relevance** | Work touches at least 3 of the 29 schisms | Can evaluate multiple claims from direct expertise |
| **C5: Response likelihood** | Known to engage with external review requests (not oversubscribed) | Practical deliverability |

Additional considerations:
- **Diversity of viewpoints:** Mix of physicists and philosophers, different interpretations of QM, different approaches to foundations.
- **Career stage:** Mix of established (higher credibility) and mid-career (higher response rate).
- **Geographic spread:** Avoid over-concentration from a single institution or school.

**Response likelihood** | Medium — less oversubscribed than senior figures, constructor theory community is actively seeking engagement |

**Why Marletto:** Constructor theory is the closest competitor framework to the needle-threading approach for S19 (nomological dualism). Marletto's classification would provide the strongest test of the taxonomy's claim that the needle-threading framework resolves S19 *better* or *differently* than constructor theory. This is a direct competitive evaluation.

**Risk:** Constructor theory is a competitor framework — there's a risk of motivated disagreement. The taxonomy package must be presented neutrally, as a classification exercise, without comparing frameworks or claiming superiority. Let the classification speak for itself.

## §4. Outreach Strategy

### 4.1 Initial Contact Template

```
Subject: Request for expert review — 29-schism taxonomy classification

Dear Professor [Name],

I am writing to invite your expert review of a taxonomy that classifies
29 unresolved conceptual tensions ("schisms") in the foundations of physics.
The taxonomy categorizes each schism as physics, philosophy, resolved, or
ill-posed, and maps each to a structural resolution.

Your expertise in [specific area] would provide an invaluable independent
perspective. The review involves:
- Classifying up to 29 schisms (estimated 5-10 within your direct expertise)
- Suggesting any missing schisms you believe should be included
- Evaluating whether any schisms you consider resolved are wrongly classified

Expected time commitment: 2-4 hours total. No prior knowledge of any
specific framework is required — we seek your independent classification.

The full taxonomy package is attached. We would be grateful for your
response within 8 weeks.

Thank you for considering this request.

[Signature]
```

## §6. Notes

1. **Contact information** should be obtained from institutional websites at time of outreach. Do not store personal email addresses in this document.

2. **The taxonomy package** (Task 2.2) must be complete before any outreach begins. The classification instructions must make it trivially easy for validators to respond — ideally a structured form with minimal free-text requirements.

3. **Anonymity:** Consider whether to offer validators the option of anonymous vs. named participation. Named responses carry more weight but may discourage candid criticism.

4. **IRB/ethics:** Classification of publicly available conceptual schisms does not constitute human subjects research in most jurisdictions. No personal data beyond professional affiliation is collected.

5. **Budget:** No honorarium budget is allocated. If a validator requires payment, escalate to user for decision.
